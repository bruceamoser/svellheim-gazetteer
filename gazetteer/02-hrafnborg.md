# Hrafnborg

Hrafnborg is the largest surviving city in Svellheim and the seat of Harald, the High King. Its timber walls overlook a sheltered inlet in the Splintered Fjords. The first settlement grew between the landing beach and a flame spring uphill. The fire that rises there, known as the Ever-Ember, still shows through the harbor fog.

## Crown, Thing, and Chainwardens

Harald directs the common defense from Hrafnborg. The Chainwardens guard the harbor and landward gates and keep the rolls that determine each household’s allowance of fuel. A household that takes in guests must register them or stretch its existing allowance. Residents can appeal a refusal at the Thing, the city’s public assembly. In the Hall of the Law-Speaker, witnesses may be called to establish what was promised, including promises made by the king.

## The spring and the market

Ember Keepers tend the Ever-Ember and keep its warm ground open to the public. The right extends to people awaiting a fuel allowance. Vows sworn at the spring invoke the fire itself as a witness. Its warmth once reached farther along the surrounding streets than it does today.

The Long-Market supplies rope, nails, cured fish, lamp oil and winter clothing. Merchants weigh **hacksilver**, pieces cut from larger silver ornaments, and also accept salt and other goods. A purchase on credit often requires a witness to the agreed price. Fuel clerks issue the slips exchanged for household wood allowances.

The temple of Thunar, god of protection, is a tall timber hall with iron-bound posts. During storms its hearth and floor are available to displaced households. Those who can work help carry water, make repairs and keep watch.

## The harbor

The **Chain-Boom** is an iron chain raised across the harbor mouth to keep out raiders and large sea creatures. Control of the chain allows the guards to detain a cargo while competing claims are heard. On the cliffs beyond it grow salt-pines whose brine-soaked timber burns blue. With the southern passage frozen, most traffic comes from neighboring harbors or inland by road.
