= Hrafnborg

Hrafnborg stands in the Splintered Fjords on Svellheim's southern coast. It is a royal seat, a refuge, and a port whose harbor can be closed with a single great iron chain. The road reaches a series of checkpoints before the city walls. Inside are ration queues, covered market stalls, and streets that grow warmer as they approach the Ever-Ember.

== Crown, Thing, and Chainwardens

Harald's title carries weight. People look to him for protection, recognize his right to offer hospitality, and invoke the king's law when they have a grievance. The Thing hears disputes and recognizes obligations in public. In the Hall of the Law-Speaker, witnesses give an oath or agreement a life beyond the people who first made it.

The Chainwardens hold authority by contract. They staff the gates, police the harbor, and enforce the permits and ration rolls that decide who enters and who draws supplies. Harald depends on them to make royal commitments effective. They depend on the city to recognize the authority under which they act. Their interests do not always coincide. A person may arrive with a promise of royal hospitality and still have to get a clerk to enter their household on the fuel roll.

== A city around a fire

The Ever-Ember is the great hearth of Hrafnborg. Appointed Embertenders keep it, and carry covered coals to relight sanctioned hearths. Most inhabitants know the flame by the warmth it gives the nearby streets and by its glow on the harbor fog. People swear vows in its presence and speak of it as a witness. Its warmth reaches less of the city than it used to.

The Long-Market sells cured fish, oil, rope, nails, and whatever winter clothes can still be patched. The scales weigh hacksilver and salt. Clerks issue fuel chits. People bargain in earshot of witnesses, since an agreement may need to be remembered long after the stall has closed.

The Temple of Thunar is a stave hall reinforced with iron. It keeps a broad hearth and opens as a shelter when households need somewhere to go. Those who can help carry water, repair the building, or take a turn on watch. Its prayers ask for the sort of protection anyone on the coast understands. Hold the door. Keep the roof on. Let the storm pass over the people inside.

== The harbor

The Chain-Boom protects the harbor from raiders and creatures that come in from the sea. The same barrier gives its keepers leverage over captains and cargo. Beyond it, salt-pines cling to the cliffs. Their wood burns blue. Much of the city's traffic now comes along the coast or inland by road. The harbor still has sailors who remember the old southern runs and can tell you what a ship used to carry back.
