= The Age of Embers

== Before the deep winter

Svellheim had short summers and hard winters long before Fimbulwinter. It also had farms worth passing down, harbors crowded with foreign cargo, and assemblies that could draw delegates from across the land. Under the High Kings, the clans came under a common law. The Alþing met at Thingvellir, and a network of roads and circular fortresses made travel and trade possible across much of the country.

The kin-strife broke the High Kings' realm into rival domains. Local assemblies and lawspeakers carried much of the old law forward, even as jarls took charge of the roads, halls, and strongholds they could still defend. Summers grew shorter. The sun paled. For generations, people built their lives around the expectation that winter would still end.

== The Long Night

At the winter solstice, Máni went red and vanished. For three days, hearths and forges went dark. When fire returned, the ice advanced, southern sea lanes closed, and more of the dead began to rise. People date the onset of Fimbulwinter from that winter. Ask what caused it and the answers multiply. Some speak of broken vows or divine judgment. Others point to the failing seasons and say the disaster had been gathering for generations.

In the third year of Fimbulwinter, people still observe the names and feasts of summer. At Harpa they mark the beginning of the planting season, even where the ground remains frozen. In Sólmánuður they remember when the great assembly met. Beyond the surviving lights, communities contend with restless dead, spoiled country, and a winter that seems to deepen wherever the land is already hurt.

== Who shares the fire

Svellheim's towns and trade roads have brought different peoples together for generations. Ancestry alone tells a stranger little about which god a household honors, what language it speaks at table, or whose word it will stand behind at the Thing.

Winter has drawn communities closer. A harbor needs fishers, smiths, preservers, and people willing to stand watch at hours when the cold can kill. Small settlements pool labor and depend on agreements with neighboring halls. Trust can still be thin. People want to know whether a neighbor kept their shift, honored their share, and spoke up when another household needed help. The same neighbors can depend on one another every day and disagree bitterly about who is entitled to the last fuel.

Heat, Weight, and Word describe much of what changes hands. Heat is food, fuel, and shelter. Weight is iron, tools, and the material means to hold a settlement together. Word is the standing that lets a promise, bargain, or claim carry force. A household can have silver in a chest and still owe its survival to someone whose word gets a door opened.

== The southern routes

The southern ports belonged to the world of merchants and sailors. Their routes and trade are part of recorded history, and the wrecks along the Weeping Coast are among the material remains of those voyages. Ice now cuts Svellheim off from those sea lanes.

Oral legends and teachings handed down in secret also speak of a Green land to the south that once traded with Svellheim. The Green land has its own place in what is remembered and taught. Accounts of the known southern ports leave its place among them unresolved.

== The gods and their people

The gods take an interest in particular lives. A few people have seen a god or felt a god's touch. Many more claim to have done so. The claims have weight in families, temples, and assemblies, where the people who heard them can spend years disagreeing about what happened and what it asks of them.

Most divine life takes place at the scale of a hearth, a crossing, or a promise. Families turn to Frigg for hospitality and shelter. Sailors make offerings to Njoror before trusting a boat to the sea. Worshippers ask Thunar to hold a roof against the storm. The gods rarely intervene in the affairs of whole realms. A person can come home convinced a god heard them while the wider country goes on enduring the same winter.
