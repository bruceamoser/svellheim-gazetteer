# Flame Springs, Shrine Fires, and the Second War

## Established setting

An Ember arises naturally from the earth at a flame spring. Cities grew around the springs, and Ember Keepers care for the Embers there. Ember Tenders keep smaller Embers burning at roadside shrines. Much of the old shrine network is lost, including sites and the original purpose of the roads it traces. Different peoples and societies preserve their own accounts of what the roads and shrines were for.

## The interwar account

See [The Years Between the Wars](../gazetteer/03-history.md#the-years-between-the-wars). The town buys the carpenter's benches for common use and authorizes Yrma to keep the approach clear with the help of the town watch. Sela's accident leads road households to support a Tender and an apprentice. Shrine peace, local hearings, and seasonal loans of a meal-house key grow from hospitality and shared maintenance. These are proposed customs spreading from particular roads and cities.

Vargeld survives below the Shield and exploits the distinction between paying for provisions and controlling admission. Lady Inga retains borrowed keys, and an agent kills an envoy under shrine peace. The seizure of grain and the rise of Vargeld's companies open the Second War. The proposed [Second War and reconstruction](../gazetteer/03-history.md#the-second-war) follows the defeat at the harvest ford, the oath of all ten peoples at Ormsund, the battle of Titan’s Stair, Vargeld’s death before both allied hosts, and the obligations to bereaved families that shaped the mixed settlements. The origins of the small Embers, the founders and original destinations of the roads, and who may move or rekindle an Ember remain open.
