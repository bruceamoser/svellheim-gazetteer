# Stories of Svellheim — author’s decisions

Discuss one story at a time. Record Bruce’s answers here in Markdown and commit them before opening the discussion for the next story.

## To live with the dead

Rann tells her own story, beginning with her earliest memory beneath a boat in her mother’s yard. At nineteen she leaves her apprenticeship with Aldis for the burial house. Her mother’s support matters later, when Rann struggles to offer Aldis the same freedom to choose her work. Their disagreement over what humans may expect of Hel begins before Aldis dies.

Rann prepares and buries her mother, then finds her home in the rocker. After Aldis leaves for Reedbank, Rann tries to release her by repaying the boat money. Leif gives Rann another way to think about the Returned before he is killed during the retreat from the raid. The shores call in their oaths after his death. Rann experiences the war through her burial work. The boat is lost in the fighting. The story ends when Rann tells her mother she is glad she came back and asks to help her find the tree for a new keel.

## The Miller’s Heel

Edda remembers a young miller whose flour enabled her to bake for a living. She knew his generosity firsthand and comforted him through nights when he heard voices. Her livelihood and her loyalty help explain why she accepts his account of the troops hidden in the caves and the private room beneath the mill.

Vargeld uses control of the grain to impose a child tax. Edda escorts children to a passage and at first believes his promises about their care. When the tax rises and none of the children return, she follows them. She recognizes what the children are being made into and resolves to tell their families. Vargeld kills her before she can bring the news home. Bruce asked for a god to exact payment and left the choice of god to the author. The manuscript uses Frigg, to whom Edda has offered bread all her working life.

## The Key in the Roof

Sif tells this story herself. She begins as a little girl making a roof tile with her uncle. Keep the goose she pressed into the clay and the extra toes she gave it. Give the two of them time together while he is well. She knows what he sounds like when he teases her, what work he trusts her to do and what she is still too small to lift. In the new telling he dies at the forest shrine. The arrest of a Tender and the murder of an envoy belong to the wider history and can happen elsewhere.

**What Sif learns on the road.** Her uncle is an Ember Tender. His livelihood takes him from one wayside shrine to the next. Sif sees him keep the small Ember burning, clear a drain, mend a roof, care for someone hurt on the road and leave dry bedding for whoever comes after him. The Keeper she knows in town cares for the fire that rises from the earth at a flame spring. The farms along her uncle’s road feed him and help provision the shelters. Through the old account of Sela breaking her hip and having to be carried back to show her neighbors the buried drain, Sif can understand how this road came to support a Tender and an apprentice. Her uncle can give her the local religious telling of the old road and admit where his knowledge ends. Each people has its own account of who laid out those roads and why the shrines stand where they do.

Let Sif see the laws work for people she meets. Even enemies who leave their weapons at the door can sleep by the same fire until morning. A grievance can be heard in daylight. A carter may borrow a key to put grain into the store. Supplying barley gives a town no claim on a traveler’s place by the fire. Sif also sees the training a person needs when traveling between those shelters, including the use of a blade and the care of wounds.

**The visitor at the first shrine.** Bruce left the god to the author. Use Hallinskidi, the Watchman who once held a crossing open until two brothers could reach safety. He speaks to Sif when she is small and tells her he has seen her tending shrines of her own. One day she will understand why anyone undertakes the work. Give them a conversation a child could have with a stranger at the door. Afterward Sif cannot decide whether she was awake, though she remembers what he said.

**The shortages and the four men.** Years later, Vargeld controls traffic through the passes. At first his men wave the Tenders through and leave the work of the shrines alone. Traveling together, Sif and her uncle meet farmers who sold their barley and people waiting for wagons recorded as having arrived. An empty store at one shrine means little by itself. Her uncle knows enough people along the road to compare what each has seen. Word gets back to Vargeld’s men that he has been telling others what he learned. Four men confront *her uncle* on the road. He tells Sif to run. When one catches her arm she remembers watching her uncle train in the yard, learning to fight on his feet, with sword and dirk and with his hands. She comes back to the fear and anger in his face. All four men die. Her uncle is wounded and has enough time left to guide her to safety.

**Two days to the Great Green.** He calls a carrier pigeon, fits a little vial into its message tube and sends it ahead. He knows the way to a shrine at the entrance to the Great Green, a place Sif thought belonged to stories. She bears some of his weight. By the second day he is pale and can walk only a little way between rests. At the shrine a Tonttu Ember Tender receives them, makes her uncle comfortable and dresses his wounds. The Tonttu are the woodland elves whose settlements lay in the old Greenweald. Her uncle asks this Tender to take Sif as an apprentice and teach her the blade, how to make a healing compress, and how to read the land and look after the people who travel it. Use *Great Green* as Sif knows the name. Leave its extent and the route to the shrine undescribed on the modern map.

**After the burial.** Before leaving with her new teacher, Sif goes to the shrine to pray. Hallinskidi asks what she has learned. Her first answer comes from the hand on her arm and what those men did to her uncle. People will hurt someone weaker for what they can get. The god grants her the truth of what she saw and asks her to remember what her uncle did during those same two days. Even as he was dying, he found someone who could care for her when he was gone. Let Sif speak about the man she has just buried. That is how she comes to understand what the god saw in her as a child, and what good one Tender can do.

## Places in the Boat

**Una volunteers.** At six, newly freed from captivity, Una made the crossing home under a rowing bench. Asa let her stay there. Years later, Una has a crew of her own. News that Vargeld has returned brings her into the war. Her first service is to carry men to Hrafnborg by sea. As the campaign moves inland, she carries troops from the southern coast up the Ormá. Ormsund, at the mouth of that river, is where the coastal and river journeys meet. Hrafnborg is a separate port in the fjords.

**The captain and the commander.** Una is fiery, loud and given to boasting. Her people love to follow her. She can take a boat through water other captains refuse to enter, and her crew have seen her make good on her word. Asa is quiet, thinks ahead and listens closely to the people she commands. By their second meeting she is directing troops in combat. Una grew up imagining the woman who carried her home. The adult she meets disappoints her, and the two clash. Give them a real disagreement over a decision for which both women will have to answer.

**The medical barge.** Una is asked to captain a barge on the Ormá carrying wounded people away from the battle at Stairwatch. Stairwatch is inland on the western approach to the mountain pass at Titan’s Stair. Bearers and wagons bring the wounded overland to a river landing where her barge can take them aboard. Una experiences the fighting through the people she receives. One possible scene for the draft is her recognizing a soldier she carried upriver when he could walk aboard for himself. She has to learn what that same journey asks of him now that he must trust her crew to lift and carry him.

**Asa’s death and Una’s oath.** Over a couple of days Una sees how Asa plans, what she does when those plans are tested and how people fare because she took the time to hear them. Una learns there is another way to lead and has reason to value it while Asa is still alive. Una witnesses Asa being killed and goes to her while they can still speak to each other. Only then does Una understand the full debt she owes her. Give the women time to say what the childhood crossing meant to each of them. Una swears an oath to the god she serves, with such conviction that she considers herself bound to it forever. Bruce left the choice of god to the author. Njoror, the Sea-King to whom island crews pray for safe harbor, is the choice for the draft. The precise words and circumstances of the oath remain for the manuscript. Keep the encounter within Una’s reach from the river. The earlier archived telling has Asa send word releasing the armies after the last prisoners are freed, which matters when placing her death.

**The last passage.** After the war Una is still transporting people, this time refugees displaced by the fighting. On that crossing she finds a small girl under a bench. End there, with Una remembering how it felt when someone spoke to her from above and how much it mattered that Asa let her stay. Give Una and the girl time for a few words of their own. What Una does for this passenger lets the reader see her keeping faith with Asa.

## Seven Notches

**Approved story direction.** Tell it from Harald’s point of view. As a boy he spent several winters with a household in the mountains. The foster brother he grew up with commands the western gate at Titan’s Stair. Before the road closures, that brother came to Hrafnborg to ask the coastal kings to help maintain the pass. Harald used his standing among them to defeat the request. He had promised Gerda that the duties from seven ships would repair the sea wall. His brother went home empty-handed.

The closure brings three armies to the pass. Harald expects his brother to yield when he sees the coastal host. Instead his brother brings an offer from the mountain kings to let Hrafnborg’s wagons through in private. If Harald accepts, the mountain soldiers holding the western gate will march east to fight the river army. His brother has a son in that company. Harald can keep his word to Gerda and get everyone he brought up the mountain home safely. His brother’s family will pay for it.

Harald owns his part in the quarrel in front of all three sides. He sends the offer to the river rulers, withdraws his soldiers while the gates are still shut, and undertakes to pay for the road out of Hrafnborg’s duties. Gerda still expects him to come home and face the families he promised a wall. The mountain rulers open both gates, and the river army stands down. End at the summer assembly. When the rulers choose someone to command their common defense, his foster brother speaks for Harald. He was there the morning Harald sent his army down the slope while his brother still held the gate.
