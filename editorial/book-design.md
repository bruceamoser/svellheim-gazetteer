# Gazetteer book plan

## Published structure

The foreword and continental map precede the history. The present-day chapters follow the history through Fimbulwinter. Each atlas area pairs a window on the approved map with a separate local illustration. Each people has a single profile page, with its homeland, a portrait and customs opposite the complete sacred origin. The calendar, language guide and cosmology close the gazetteer.

Give each short story in the companion volume *Stories of Svellheim* its own title, viewpoint, and chapters. Introduce a household before the event that will change it. A remembered sound or a quarrel over something both characters have handled can show a reader what they stand to lose. Keep the source tellings in the manuscript archive. The gazetteer follows how events changed the world. Sacred origin stories belong with their peoples in the main book.

## Narrative voice

Use “strange,” “unfamiliar,” or the character’s own reaction in place of “queer” throughout the Svellheim books.

Read each chapter as a possible first visit. At the first mention of a custom, show what someone actually does and what another person can count on as a result. For a flame spring, begin with the fire rising from the ground and the people who build beside it. The title Ember Keeper makes sense once the reader has seen the work. When a character acts on an old promise, recall who made it and what keeping it will cost here. Let unresolved questions belong to the people of Svellheim.

Write with the authority of a setting reference. Open chapters and major entries on a place, object, or custom the reader can picture, then explain what it reveals about the world. Move between the scale of kingdoms and the scale of households. An old fort can carry the story of a lost kingdom, and the price of charcoal can carry the story of a burned forest. Let named places do that work where the sources support them.

Use plain, specific language and a measured cadence. Name the people who hold an opinion when accounts disagree. Use uncertainty where it belongs to Svellheim's history, especially in the earliest settlement years and the cause of Fimbulwinter. Bring the prosperous years to life through work, travel, feasts, and ordinary comforts. Give the present room for fellowship, civic pride, and devotion as well as hunger.

## Page design

Use the gold interlace around each cover. In the gazetteer, repeat it in the footer and place transparent illustrations of objects from the text in the space at section endings. Set the companion like a paperback novel, with one column on a six-by-nine-inch page, a plain folio, indented prose and a fresh page for each story. Its only illustration is the cover.

Prototype two US Letter spreads before settling trim or page count. Use a full-width timeline or map where readers need to follow information across the page. Test a two-column serif setting for place entries, with clean sans-serif headings, generous margins, and running heads naming the region. Check Þ, ð, Á, ø, and other characters at body and caption sizes.

Use warm off-white text pages, charcoal ink, muted iron blue for maps, and ember orange for a few accents. Draw the book's character from harbor ironwork, timber carving, and marks on ledgers. Keep text backgrounds quiet. Build a searchable PDF with linked contents, bookmarks, selectable text, embedded fonts, and a place-name index. Check rendered pages at screen size and printed scale.

## Artwork and cartography

| Priority | Artwork |
| --- | --- |
| Continental map | Use the existing atlas grid to establish coastlines, the three belts, rivers and lakes, major settlements, and routes. Label known geography and keep the Green land in the oral record until its relationship to the known south is settled. |
| Historical opening | Show the thriving Silver Era through harbor traffic, stored grain, trade, and mixed communities. A companion illustration of the same coast under winter would make the loss visible. |
| Hrafnborg spread | Commission a harbor panorama with the chain-boom, timber defenses, boats, and signs of daily work. Pair it with a legible city plan locating the Long-Market, Hall of the Law-Speaker, Temple of Thunar, and the public approaches to the harbor. |
| Regional plates | Establish the three belts with materially distinct scenes. Show fishing and preservation on the coast, iron and charcoal labor inland, and steam and exposed ice in the north. |
| Spot illustrations | Use a ration chit, a salt-pine branch, an oath ring, a broken trade seal, or a fragment of the drowned names tapestry where the objects help explain the text. |

Use original art and cartography. Record creator, usage rights, and credit for each asset. Keep high-resolution originals for print and export lighter copies for the reading PDF. Start production with the history and Hrafnborg sample spreads to test the timeline, maps, type, and art together.
