# Foreword

Svellheim’s grain roads once connected farms beneath the central mountains to ships on the southern sea. Generations of war and reconstruction made those routes possible. The kings who finally agreed to keep them open gave their peoples a prosperity still remembered as the Silver Era.

This book begins with the traditions that precede written history and follows the rise of the kingdoms to Fimbulwinter, the winter that has outlasted summer. The Age of Embers is the present day, three years into the cold. The roads, shrines and agreements described here belong to people who must still make a living among the remains of a more prosperous world.

The peoples of Svellheim tell different accounts of their beginnings. Their sacred stories appear beside the descriptions of their homelands and customs. The companion volume Stories of Svellheim follows a boatwright and her daughter at Reedbank, the people caught up in the wars, and the families making homes along the coast in Harald’s time.
