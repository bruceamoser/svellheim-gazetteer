# From the Author

I never meant to write a book of stories. When I started building Svellheim, I kept notes about its history and the way people lived there. I had two towns on opposite shores of a lake and a quarrel that would grow into a war. Elsewhere I had a few lines about how a family prepared someone they loved for burial. Those pieces came together when I began to wonder about the woman who did that work. Who would she turn to when it came time to bury her own mother?

That was how I found Rann. She got hold of my heart the day I pictured her lying in the shavings under a boat. She poked a finger through a hole in a plank, and her mother, busy on the other side, stopped to kiss it. Of all the things Rann could remember about growing up in a boatyard, that was the one she wanted to tell me first.

Writing about the two of them took me back to times with my own mum that I hadn’t thought about in years. There’s a bit where Rann comes home and looks over at her mother’s chair, the way she’s done all her life. I think that was the closest I came to putting into words how an ordinary day with someone you love can be the very thing you’d give anything to have again.

After Rann, the rest came pouring out. In my notes about the roads I found a little girl trying to keep up with her uncle. In the long winter I found a boy still waiting for his father to come home. I owe Rann these seven stories. I owe her, too, for the time I got to spend remembering my mum.

Thanks mum
*- Allan*
